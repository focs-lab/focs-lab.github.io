The Foundations of Computer Systems (FOCS) Lab at the National University of Singapore is hiring postdoctoral researchers (Research Fellows) and research assistants to work on concurrency in Go and on safe and secure hardware. You will work with Temasek Presidential Young Professor Umang Mathur and the lab's researchers and students at the NUS School of Computing.

## Concurrent Go programs

A Go test may pass under one ordering of goroutines and fail under another. We study which executions Go permits, how to recognise bugs from observed executions, and how to exercise schedules that ordinary tests can miss. We work with international researchers and Go developers on the semantics of concurrency and on tools for testing real programs.

### Research Fellow — Analysis and Verification of Concurrent Go Programs {#go-fellow}

You will formalize aspects of Go's memory model and concurrency semantics and develop algorithms for data race detection, predictive testing, or model checking. You will implement research prototypes, design experiments to evaluate them, publish the results, and help mentor students.

You should have a PhD in Computer Science or a closely related discipline by the time you start, strong programming skills, experience building research prototypes, and good written and oral communication skills. We welcome applicants with a background in formal methods, systems, or programming languages. Familiarity with Go, concurrency, program analysis, or automated verification is desirable.

### Research Assistant — Analysis and Testing of Concurrent Go Programs {#go-assistant}

You will instrument Go programs, build benchmarks, investigate concurrency bugs, and evaluate how well our analyses and tests detect them. You will also contribute to a formalization of Go's memory model and concurrency semantics in a proof assistant and document your implementations and experimental results.

You should have a bachelor's or master's degree in Computer Science, Computer Engineering, or a closely related discipline. Familiarity with Go, concurrent programming, or program analysis is desirable.

Explore the [lab's concurrency research](https://focs-lab.comp.nus.edu.sg/projects/concurrency/), try [GCT, our controlled concurrency testing tool for Go](https://github.com/focs-lab/gct), or read [The Complexity of Testing Message-Passing Concurrency (POPL 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3776643).

## Safe and secure hardware

When hardware components exchange data, both sides must agree on when the data is valid and when it can change. We design languages and verification tools that let hardware designers express these assumptions and check them. We work with international researchers and hardware designers on type systems, compiler correctness, automated verification, and ways to detect and prevent timing and security vulnerabilities.

### Research Fellow — Formal Methods for Safe and Secure Hardware {#hardware-fellow}

You will develop hardware languages, compilers, or verification algorithms and evaluate them on realistic hardware designs. You will publish your findings and help mentor students.

You should have a PhD in Computer Science, Mathematics, Electrical and Computer Engineering, or a closely related discipline by the time you start, strong technical and implementation skills, and good written and oral communication skills. Relevant specialisations include formal methods, systems, programming languages, hardware, and computer architecture in Computer Science, logic in Mathematics, and computer architecture in Electrical and Computer Engineering. We welcome applicants whose experience is primarily in programming languages or formal methods, including those new to hardware.

### Research Assistant — Formal Methods for Safe and Secure Hardware {#hardware-assistant}

You will implement tools for hardware design, analysis, testing, and verification. You will contribute to hardware languages, compilers, or automated reasoning techniques, build benchmark suites, and document how the tools perform on realistic designs.

You should have a bachelor's or master's degree in Computer Science, Computer Engineering, Electrical Engineering, or a closely related discipline. Familiarity with digital design or hardware description languages is desirable. We also welcome strong programmers who are new to hardware.

Explore the [lab's hardware research](https://focs-lab.comp.nus.edu.sg/projects/secure-hardware-design/) and [AnvilHDL compilers, formalizations, and benchmarks](https://github.com/AnvilHDL). Read [Anvil: A General-Purpose Timing-Safe Hardware Description Language (ASPLOS 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3779212.3790125) and [A Type System for Communication-Safe Hardware Design (POPL 2027, to appear)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26599).

## Experience across the projects

In the Go project, we will design and evaluate agentic workflows for program analysis, testing, and verification. In the hardware project, we will investigate how these workflows can help us design, analyse, and verify circuits. Experience with such workflows, compilers, SMT solvers, or proof assistants such as Lean, Rocq, and Isabelle/HOL is welcome. Experience with LLVM or sanitizers such as AddressSanitizer and particularly ThreadSanitizer is also useful.

For both Research Assistant roles, we look for strong programming skills, sound knowledge of algorithms and data structures, and experience developing, debugging, and testing software. You should be able to work independently and communicate technical ideas clearly. You can apply with or without prior research experience.
